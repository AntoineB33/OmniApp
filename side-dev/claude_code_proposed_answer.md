
## An answer for test 12 implementation

They are all of the same kind — the schedule is exact near the line and provisional far from it, which is the shape the pace requirement above describes.

1. **Past `local_end`, the display draws the standing chain.** The rules at the line are sound as far as the plan's own prefix reaches, capped by the 6-hour lookahead it was shown the environment over — in practice one to six hours ahead of the line, where the requirement asks for ten minutes. Beyond that the display continues with the chain derived for the environment standing (no break reached), which was not seeded with the local rules' output: the far end is therefore the provisional part that "goes on changing", not a wrong answer claimed as final.
2. **The rules at a position are derived when the line STOPS there.** A regime costs a second or two (worst measured 3.6 s, against the 10 s budget) and is then kept, so a position visited once is free forever after. Playing the sweep crosses a regime every frame, far faster than they can be derived, so while the line is moving none are: the display carries the standing chain on as the provisional answer and the line runs smoothly, and the position the sweep is paused at (or clicked on, or dragged to) becomes exact within a frame or two. This is the "cache at anchors, recompute the local window" shape: a derivation is bounded by the bracket around $t_p$ — and by a 3-second budget past which the rules are answered for that one position exactly rather than for a range — never by the three days.
3. **The rounding epsilon.** A slot that has shrunk below it is dropped from the rules (the epsilon this document already asks for), so the drawn local part can end a sliver short of the plan's own reach; the chain takes over exactly there rather than leaving a sliver of nothing.
4. **A frame that is drawn is not a frame that is seen.** Tk repaints from an idle handler, and an idle handler runs only when no timer is due — so four panels whose `after` loops are perpetually overdue starve the idle queue *permanently*, and the window opens, is drawn into, and shows nothing at all for as long as it runs. Two rules keep that from coming back, and both are in `MovingCasePanel._tick`: a frame ends by **presenting** what it drew (`update_idletasks`, idle events only, so no user callback can re-enter the tick), and it draws **only what changed** — a paused panel rebuilding every rectangle of its timeline sixty times a second is what left no room to present anything. Measured while both chains derive: the window paints two to four times a second, and once they are settled it is idle.

**And nothing is derived before the window opens.** `uv run tests_displayer.py` is the display run: it creates the window first (on screen in ~0.1 s), builds tests 1-11 behind the bar that says so, and prints test 12's statement *without* settling it — the chain is then derived inside the panel, a link at a time between frames, which is what makes the paragraph above literally true. The checks belong to the verification run, `uv run tests_displayer.py --verify`, because `verify_progressive` calls `pw.settle()`: two and a half minutes of work that would both delay the window by that long and hand it a chain with nothing left to derive. The display is built in stages that are each PUT ON SCREEN before the next is built — tests 1-10, then tests 10-11's fitted regimes, then test 12's panel — so what is finished is readable while the rest is still being scheduled.







## An answer for test 13 implementation

The percentages are a property of the POSITION a plan is made from, and of nothing else. That one sentence is the whole design, and it is what keeps the answer the same kind of object as test 12's: a plan is a statement about the schedule from one position, so how the targets go on moving after it is the next position's business, and a rule list never has to carry a time-varying percentage inside itself.

1. **The seam is one function.** `ProgressiveWindow` takes a `blend(t) -> tasks`, and `_sched_at(t)` builds the scheduler with the percentages at t. The rules at the line come from `_sched_at(t_p)`; a link of the chain made from g comes from `_sched_at(g)`. Everything else -- the drag and the merge, the chain settled from t=0 outward, the affine regimes at the line, the frozen past, the pace -- is test 12's, untouched. A blend that returns the same tasks at every position gives test 12 back link for link, which is checked rather than asserted (`check_blend_transparency`).

2. **The two states.** Task A hands half of its share to the ten privileged tasks: A 50% -> 25%, each privileged 2.5% -> 5%, the ten ordinary ones unchanged at 2.5%. The second state is reached at $t_p$=48h and **held** after it, not extrapolated -- a percentage is a share of a hundred, and a line drawn past the state it was fitted to takes one of them negative on the third day. Holding it is also what makes the schedule continuous at 48h rather than kinked into nonsense.

3. **Where the line stands, and where the blend is seen.** $t_p$ starts at 24h and sweeps to 72h, exactly as in test 12 -- the first day's breaks are the static ones the line never reaches, so it cannot start before them. The blend is not half-hidden by that: the first half of it is what the chain from t=0 is made with, link by link, and that is the definitive part drawn to the LEFT of the line; the sweep carries the second half at the line itself, and past 48h the second state simply stands. The panel's status bar names the percentages in force at the position the line is at.

4. **The scale of the compensation does not move with the blend.** The ordinary tasks keep their 2.5%, so the minimal period $\max(m_i/p_i)$ -- and with it the field's decay constant tau, and the displayer's tau knob -- is the same 45min/2.5% at every position. The blend changes who the timeline is for, not the scale the compensation works at.

5. **The rules at the line are still affine in $t_p$, and the derivation is now bounded from the inside.** Between two breakpoints the percentages drift slowly enough that the fit that held for test 12 still holds; where the drift moves a breakpoint, it is the same bisection that finds it. It does fail more often, though, and the search then reaches more often for the one expensive step -- the bisection on the fit itself, whose every round is a whole `_fit`, ten-odd plans. Consulted only between rounds, the three-second budget was overrun by whatever round it was in the middle of: 12 s measured, against a requirement of ten. It is now a **deadline** the search honours from the inside (`SlidingRules._out_of_time`), so past it the search stops NARROWING -- it never stops answering, and a regime is verified before it is kept, so all that is given up is the range the rules are claimed for. Being wall-clock rather than a work budget, it bounds the derivation on a slow machine exactly as on a fast one: worst measured 6.1 s for test 13 and 3.3 s for test 12, with one plan (a tenth of a second) the only thing that can follow it.

6. **What is checked.** Everything test 12 owes (the recurrence rules, the drag and merge, the resume contract -- with the percentages held fixed across each pair, for the same reason the environment is --, the pace, the periods, the minimums, the frozen past, and that the rules reproduce the scheduler at positions they were never fitted on), plus `check_priority_blend`: the blend starts at the first state, reaches the second at exactly 48h, is affine in between and held after; the plan at the line is the plan those percentages give, rebuilt from a scheduler constructed out of `tasks_at(t_p)` alone; and at some sampled positions it really differs from what the starting percentages would give -- without that last one a blend the scheduler never read would pass every other check.

7. **What the shares do and do not promise.** The guarantee is per position: the plan made at $t_p$ satisfies the percentages at $t_p$. It is not that a window of the timeline measures them -- the pool converges over its minimal period, thirty hours here, which is the same order as the blend itself, so a twelve-hour window follows the blend only loosely. The reported line therefore quotes both ends of the target (`A 50% -> 25%`) rather than pretending to one number: over the three days A takes 24.2% of the schedulable time where test 12 gave it 37.6%, and the privileged ten rise from 34.1% to 36.1%.
